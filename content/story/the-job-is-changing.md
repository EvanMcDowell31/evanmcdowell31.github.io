The way we build software is changing faster than any of us planned for. You can feel it in every standup and every PR.

There are two ways to meet that. You can wait for the change to settle and hope your old habits still fit. Or you can lean in before it feels comfortable.

This summer I leaned in.

## What leaning in looked like

I'm a contractor on the Walmart frontend, the team behind Walmart's healthier experience. I stopped treating AI as a chat box and started building around it. Skills instead of one-off prompts. Parallel agents instead of one conversation. Predictable tools for Jira, Slack, PRs and more. Then I made it lean, so every skill runs under a tight token budget.

I measured myself: merged product PRs per month, compared against Walmart's FTEs. Before, I sat between the 74th and 85th percentile. July dipped to the 57th, while I was learning iOS and learning to build with AI at the same time. In September I hit the 99.95th. Across August and September I averaged about 9× my typical month from the year before.

The change went past my own output. That work became 100+ skills in five bundles, open to my whole team, plus Wibey Fleet, now on the Wibey plugin marketplace for anyone. It got me shipping on Glass iOS and Android, two codebases I had never touched. And I still wrote 178 of the 300 commits in our 263-test production regression suite.
